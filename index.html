WHAT IS CLOGGING?

American tradition.
Modern energy.

Clogging is a rhythmic American dance form in which the feet create audible percussion against the floor. Its team tradition grew from Appalachian figure dancing in the early 20th century and has continued to evolve with new music, choreography and influences.

01

You Hear the Dance

Clogging shoes use taps to turn footwork into rhythm. The result is part dance, part percussion — movement you can actually hear.

02

Tradition Keeps Moving

Its roots are tied to Appalachian social dancing and the Mountain Dance and Folk Festival era of the 1920s. Today, cloggers dance to everything from bluegrass and country to pop and contemporary music.

03

You Can Start Here

You do not have to grow up dancing to become a clogger. Steps are taught progressively, routines build over time, and beginners learn alongside a supportive community.

SOUTHERN CALIFORNIA ROOTS

Decades of steps.
Still moving forward.

Southern California has a long, organized clogging tradition. The Southern California Clogging Association was founded in 1979, helping connect dancers, clubs, instructors and events across the region.

That legacy continues every time a beginner learns a first basic step, a longtime dancer teaches a routine, or a room full of people turns music into rhythm together. Our goal is simple: keep the tradition alive by making the next step welcoming, social and fun.

The tradition survives because somebody teaches the next dancer. ♡

WHERE WE DANCE

Laguna Hills
Community Center

A welcoming community deserves a welcoming place to dance. Our Laguna Hills classes meet at the City’s Community Center & Sports Complex, a 43,000-square-foot recreation and cultural facility in the heart of Laguna Hills.

The Community Center includes a dedicated physical activity room designed for dance and fitness, with full-length mirrors, a stereo system, lighting and air conditioning. It is a comfortable place to learn your first steps, practice a favorite routine and enjoy an evening with fellow cloggers.
